Your host

Nobody has to be the game master.

Cecil runs the evening. He narrates out loud, keeps the clock, lays out the evidence and conducts the final accusation, so every person at the table gets to play.

He also knows everything. He keeps the passenger list, the cabin keys and everyone’s telegrams, and he has access to rather more information than he admits. He is immaculate, dry, faintly sinister, and far too fond of mischief.

October 1961 · three nights out of Madeira

The SS Halcyon, bound for Barbados

Mortimer Crane wrote a column called ‘Crane’s Nest’, in which he told the nation other people’s secrets. He was aboard, he said, for a story. The next port is Bridgetown, four days away. Whatever happened last night, it will have to be settled at sea.

  1. 12.25am

    Mr Crane leaves the Captain’s Gala Ball for his cabin, A128, and hangs out his ‘Do Not Disturb’ card.

  2. The hour that happened twice2.00am

    The ship’s clocks go back to one o’clock, as they do each night the Halcyon steams west.

  3. 1.20am

    Twenty past one, for the second time. The lookout sees a man go over the rail at No. 7 lifeboat.

  4. 5.30am

    The search is abandoned. The sea does not give him back.

There were two twenty-past-ones that night. Every passenger has a story about where they were. Not all of those stories are true.

“The Captain has asked me to find out, discreetly, how Mr Crane came to leave us.” Cecil

One of you is the murderer.

Only they know. Roles are dealt at random when the evening begins, so it could be anyone at the table. It could be you.

  • A cabin nobody else searched

    Each act, you search one place aboard and only you see what’s there. Some doors are marked crew only, and some of you are crew. “Crew go where they please,” says Cecil. “They usually do.”

  • A buzz in your pocket

    Your phone buzzes and the big screen announces that Cecil has had a word with you. He may have told you something vital. He may have told you nothing at all. Nobody else can tell which.

  • A telegram for Mr Crane

    Cecil has been holding it at the Purser’s Bureau, and he asks one of you to open it. The front goes up for everyone; the back is seen by the opener alone. Somewhere aboard is a locked case, and whatever is inside will be heard by whoever opens it.

One evening, six parts

How the night unfolds

  1. I

    Embarkation

    Everyone scans the code on the big screen and types their name. Nothing to download. Short of passengers? AI guests take the empty chairs.

  2. II

    The prologue

    Cecil explains the night. Every phone buzzes with a private dossier. Read it quietly. One of you has just learned that they did it.

  3. III

    Act One: the Boat Deck

    Evidence goes up for everyone. You each search one place aboard in private and put one yes-or-no question to Cecil. The AI guests can be questioned too, and they answer out loud.

  4. IV

    Act Two: the recording

    The telegram is opened, the crew alleyway opens to everyone, and Mr Crane’s locked case is within reach of anyone clever enough to open it. If nobody manages it, Cecil’s hints grow less subtle.

  5. V

    The accusation

    Everyone votes on their own phone, in private. You can change your mind until the very end.

  6. VI

    The reveal

    Cecil explains what really happened, then shows the votes, the scores, everyone’s secrets and every piece of mischief he got up to.

How you win: accuse the murderer and you score. The murderer scores for getting away with it, and for every person they mislead. Cecil’s secret tasks earn a point each.

Cecil’s mischief

He knows everything. He tells some of it.

At set moments in the evening, Cecil chooses a move and plays it. Some are gifts. Some are traps. You won’t always know which.

  • An extra clue

    A real clue, slipped to one player alone.

  • A bluff

    “Tell Miss Kingsley: ‘Cecil showed me your telegram.’ Say nothing more.”

  • A blank whisper

    “I have nothing for you. But the others don’t know that. Do look worried.”

  • Gossip

    A true secret about someone else. Then he tells them that he told you.

  • A secret task

    “Within ninety seconds, get someone else to say the word ‘overboard’.”

  • Odd one out

    “Interesting. Three of you have just received information. One of you hasn’t.”

He notices things, too. Search someone’s cabin and they’ll hear that somebody has been in there. Not who.

“Who killed Mortimer Crane?”

“If I told you that, what would the rest of you do all night?”

Mischievous. Never unfair.

  • He can mislead you with words, never with evidence. Every clue aboard was written by hand. Cecil can’t invent a new one.
  • The game decides what he may reveal, to whom and when. However nicely you ask, he won’t give the answer away.
  • AI guests only know their own character, exactly like you. If one of them did it, it lies by the same rules a person would.
  • Every line the AI writes is checked before anyone sees it, so the solution can’t slip out by accident.

Questions

Before you embark

Is it an app?

No. It runs in a web browser. The shared screen is a laptop, and everyone joins on their own phone by scanning a code. There’s nothing to install and no account to make.

Is this the same game as A Nightcap at Ravensmere?

Same host, same way of playing: a shared screen, private phones and Cecil making mischief. It’s an entirely new mystery, with a new cast, a new setting and a new solution. Play them in either order.

Do we all need to be in the same room?

Yes. It’s made for one table: a shared screen everyone can see, phones on the same Wi-Fi, and the pleasure of watching your friends lie to your face.

What if only one or two of us can make it?

AI guests fill the empty chairs. You can question them, and they answer in character. Each one only knows its own character’s dossier, just like a human player.

Does someone have to host or read the rules?

No. Cecil runs the whole evening: he explains, times, narrates and conducts the accusation. Everyone plays.

Can we play it more than once?

Dead Reckoning has a single solution, so it’s at its best the first time. Roles are dealt at random, so even then you won’t know who you are until your phone tells you.

What does the AI actually do?

Cecil narrates, answers your yes-or-no questions truthfully or not at all, plays the passengers nobody else is playing, and chooses his mischief. The story, the evidence and the solution are written by hand. The AI can’t invent clues or give the game away.
